| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Founder capacity:** about 21 cohorts plus content plus sales in Year 1 | High | High | Contract facilitators from Q3 of Y1; teaching assistants; record the Academy early | Founder |
| 2 | **Founder dependency** puts off buyers and caps the valuation | High | High | Facilitator licensing, company-owned channels, founder-touch KPI | Founder → GM |
| 3 | **Recurring share** stays around 51% instead of 55% | Medium | High | CapabilityOS annual plans, Guild bundles, annual billing, multi-year B2B ([options](/business-model/unit-economics#closing-the-recurring-gap)) | GM |
| 4 | **CAC** stays above $180 | Medium | Medium | Free workshop, referral programme, partner co-promotion | Growth |
| 5 | **Completion and quality slip** as facilitators scale | Medium | High | Certification gates, cohort dashboards, 12-seat cap | Programme |
| 6 | **Enterprise sales cycles** longer than planned | Medium | Medium | Paid workshop pilots, 3× pipeline coverage | B2B |
| 7 | **AI tooling changes fast**, so curriculum goes stale | High | Medium | Problem-first design (built on problems, not tools); quarterly refresh | Content |
| 8 | **Brand spread**: four brands dilute attention | Medium | Medium | The Hub as single front door; Studios held to 10% or less of revenue | Founder |
| 9 | **Merch** becomes a distraction | Low | Low | Hard cap at 6% of revenue | Growth |
| 10 | **CapabilityOS cannibalises** the $497 Academy and the Guild | Medium | Medium | Clear positioning (daily habit vs course vs practitioner community); decide the Academy/CapabilityOS split | Founder |
| 11 | **CapabilityOS renewals** fall below 50% | Medium | Medium | Projects and live Q&A in year 1; upgrade offers into Certification and the Guild | CapabilityOS product lead |
